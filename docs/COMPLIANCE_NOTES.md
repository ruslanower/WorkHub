# Regulatory, Legal & Compliance Architecture

This document provides legal, tax, AML, and data protection guidance across the jurisdictions supported by **WorkHub**.

---

## 1. Protected Payments & Escrow Licensing (PSD2 / Banking Rails)

### The Legal Problem
Under European Union regulations (Payment Services Directive 2 — **PSD2**, and upcoming **PSD3**), Ukrainian financial services legislation, and Kenyan Central Bank rules, a digital platform that accepts funds from buyers and subsequently pays sellers is classified as providing **payment services** (money remittance and execution of payment transactions).

Operating a direct escrow balance sheet without a licensed entity exposes the marketplace to severe criminal and regulatory penalties for unlicensed banking activities. The "commercial agent exemption" is heavily restricted when platforms act for both buyer and seller.

### The WorkHub Compliance Architecture
WorkHub never takes legal custody of transaction funds. Instead, it integrates with **accredited, licensed Payment Service Providers (PSPs)** or banks:
1. **Segregated Safeguarding Accounts**: Customer funds are routed directly from the buyer’s card or bank account into a designated safeguarding client account managed by the licensed PSP.
2. **Conditional Release Authorizations**: WorkHub provides only the algorithmic and contractual signals (e.g. digital completion act signed) directing the PSP to execute the release.
3. **Bankruptcy Remoteness**: If WorkHub were ever to experience corporate insolvency, customer escrow funds remain isolated in segregated client trust accounts and cannot be touched by platform creditors.

## 2. Independent Contractor Classification & Labor Law

### Preventing Misclassification Risks
Physical labor marketplaces face continuous regulatory scrutiny regarding whether independent workers are de facto employees. To protect the platform and contractors across common law and civil law jurisdictions:

* **No Fixed Schedule or Exclusivity**: Workers maintain complete autonomy over when, where, and how often they log into WorkHub. No minimum hours are mandated.
* **Tools and Equipment**: Contracts explicitly stipulate that independent contractors provide their own tools, protective gear, and materials, unless specifically supplied by the client as part of the job scope.
* **Pricing Freedom**: Workers submit their own binding bids (`Offer.price`). Platform price guidance (`priceGuidance()`) is provided strictly as non-binding statistical information based on recent historical averages.
* **Multi-App Freedom**: Nothing restricts workers from participating concurrently on other marketplaces or engaging direct offline clients.

## 3. Tax Transparency & Platform Reporting (DAC7 / FOP / KRA)

### European Union (Poland Pack — `PL`)
* **DAC7 Compliance (Directive 2021/514)**: Digital platforms facilitating physical services within the EU must collect, verify, and annually report seller identification, tax identification numbers (NIP), bank account details, and quarterly turnover to the tax authorities of the relevant Member State.
* **Unregistered Activity (*Działalność nierejestrowana*)**: In Poland, individuals earning below the monthly statutory threshold (75% of minimum wage) are permitted to operate without formal JDG business registration. WorkHub tracks monthly cumulative volume to notify workers when registration becomes mandatory.

### Ukraine (`UA`)
* **Simplified Tax System (*FOP* Groups 2 & 3)**: Independent tradespeople commonly operate as Sole Proprietors (*FOP*). Bilateral service agreements and completion acts generated by WorkHub provide the mandatory primary accounting documentation (*первинні документи*) required for tax audits under the Ukrainian Tax Code.

### Kenya (`KE`)
* **KRA eTIMS & Digital Service Tax**: Integration with Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) electronic tax invoicing requirements and mobile money transaction reference logging ensures transparent accounting for sole traders.

## 4. Privacy & Zero-Retention Identity (GDPR / Data Minimization)

### Zero-Retention Principle
Traditional identity verification systems that store scanned passports, national ID cards, and biometric facial scans create immense cybersecurity liabilities and violate GDPR's **Article 5(1)(c) (Data Minimization)**.

WorkHub operates on a zero-retention model:
1. Verification is offloaded to accredited identity partners (e.g. state digital ID rails such as Diia or mObywatel, or certified biometric IDV partners).
2. The third-party provider issues a signed, time-limited cryptographic token affirming that the user has satisfied the verification tier (L1, L2, or L3).
3. WorkHub stores **only the verification level, issuance timestamp, and partner token reference**. No raw photographs, biometric vectors, or national identity card images are retained in WorkHub databases.

### Contact Masking as a Privacy Shield
Customer and worker phone numbers, emails, and messenger handles are systematically masked prior to formal contract signing and escrow funding:
* Prevents unsolicited off-platform harassment.
* Ensures platform communication logs remain admissible in the event of a dispute.
* Enforces GDPR privacy guarantees before personal contact details are legitimately exchanged for on-site arrival.

## 5. Legal Enforceability of Contracts & Completion Acts

### Electronic Contracting & Admissibility
* **Bilateral Agreements**: Executed via electronic acceptance (*clickwrap*) upon offer acceptance and escrow reservation. The platform records mutual consent, timestamp, IP hash, and identity verification level.
* **Completion Acts (*Protokół Odbioru* / *Акт Виконаних Робіт*)**: Bilateral confirmation with deliverable photographic evidence, timestamped check-in, and check-out records creates a binding audit trail that significantly reduces frivolous chargebacks and provides conclusive proof during mediation.
